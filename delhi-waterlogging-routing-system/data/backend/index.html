        About & how it works

        Delhi JalAlert is a student project for waterlogging awareness and route planning.

        How it works

        1. Find routes. A routing service (OSRM or openrouteservice) returns up to three alternative road routes.
        2. Score the routes. The backend model estimates waterlogging risk for each section of each route.
        3. Compare. The app colours each section and ranks routes using simple, visible rules.

        What the labels mean

        Model-predicted
        An estimate from the machine-learning model. It is not an observation.
        Forecast
        Expected rain or conditions in the future. Never shown as observed rainfall.
        Live observation
        A measurement of current conditions from a named source.
        Historical record
        A past incident. It does not mean the place is flooded now.
        Unverified report
        A citizen observation that nobody has checked yet.
        Unknown
        No prediction available. Shown in grey, and never treated as low risk.

        How routes are ranked

        • Fastest: shortest travel time first.
        • Lowest risk: lowest length-weighted average risk score first; ties go to the quicker route.
        • Balanced: equal weight on travel time and risk score, each scaled across the routes found.
        • Routes without a risk assessment are listed after assessed routes.

        Limits to keep in mind

        • Estimates can be wrong and conditions change quickly. A lower-risk route is not a guaranteed safe route.
        • The model score is shown as a “risk score”, not a probability, unless the backend says it is calibrated.
        • The routing service finds roads; it does not know which ones are flooded.
        • Two-wheeler routes currently use the car road network.
